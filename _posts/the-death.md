I: The Death

<!--Prologue: The Mute Genius-->
Something has always been wrong with me.

It began in my early childhood, with a silence. I would often find myself unable to speak, to answer a simple question. No one knew that my silence wasn't born of ignorance. It was born of a terrifying, paralyzing clarity. I had discovered that for every question asked, for every statement made, there existed not one, but a thousand, a million possible responses. And once I saw this, the mechanical, pre-programmed replies that others seemed to produce so effortlessly felt meaningless. But I didn't yet know how to find the optimal response, the *one true answer* in that infinite sea of possibilities. So, most of the time, I was simply mute.

I remember a girl in my class, Ah Sum, who liked to play with me. I was maybe seven or eight. One day, she came up to me, her expression a complex mixture of curiosity and possessiveness. "What do you like about Ah Yan?" she asked, naming another girl in our class.

Her question sent my young mind into a tailspin. *Like?* I talked to Ah Yan, yes, but what did it mean to "like" her? And why was Ah Sum asking? Her pouting lips, the slight furrow of her brow—it was jealousy. She was operating on an assumption that I liked Ah Yan, and that this was a problem.

My mind exploded into a thousand branching pathways. Which part of this complex social equation should I address first? The flawed premise that I "liked" Ah Yan? Or the deeper question of why my "liking" her would even be an issue for Ah Sum? Should I clarify that I had never even considered the concept of "liking"? Or should I counter-question her, asking for a precise definition of the term? Or perhaps I should tackle her baseless jealousy, or the even more fundamental irrationality of a child's possessiveness? Each path led to another, which in turn led to a higher-level value judgment. An infinite, recursive loop of justification. My brain, a tiny, overclocked engine, was running at maximum capacity.

On the outside, I was just a boy, staring blankly, mute.

Occasionally, a pre-calculated, charming phrase would escape my lips. But more often, what came out was met with laughter and ridicule. My operating system was not compatible with the world's. This thing inside me, this ability to deconstruct every interaction to its meta-structural core, was not a gift. It was a curse. And it was just the beginning of my alienation.
